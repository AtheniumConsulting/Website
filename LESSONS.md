# Lessons learned

Recorded on 9 November 2026 when the first build was removed.

## What happened

Over one long session the work produced a scaffold, CI, tests, Sanity schemas, a design contract, a direction process, framework research and a landing page, across seven stacked pull requests. The owner rejected the first two landing pages on sight. One was too literal to the brand system with no taste. The next was restrained to the point of looking like a wireframe.

## Lessons

1. Show a picture first. The owner judges a design by looking at it, so a visual comp comes before any document, rule set or code.
2. Do not build infrastructure before one design is approved. Tests, CI, schemas and registries add weight and no value until a page is liked.
3. Rules can produce a flat page. A direction that bans imagery, gradients and motion yields a wireframe. Test the direction against the screen early.
4. Placeholders are not a design. Orange brackets across a page read as unfinished. Use realistic sample content, flagged as sample, to judge the design.
5. Name the missing inputs at the start. Reference sites, approved photography and positioning were missing and belong to others. Ask for them, and do not substitute process.
6. Keep volume proportionate. Seven pull requests of notes and frameworks before one pleasing page was too much. Prefer one small thing the owner can react to.
7. Admired sites in this space rely on real photography under a dark wash, a short headline and strong scale.

## Exception recorded on 24 November 2026

The 11 December launch date left no room to wait for an approved design before setting up the project. The owner approved a bare Next.js skeleton for T118: Lato, brand colours and a holding page. Lesson 2 still applies to everything else. Tests, CI and schemas wait for an approved design.
